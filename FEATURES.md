# Operix ERP — What It Does For Your Business

This is a plain-language guide to every module in the system: what it is,
and what it actually does for an ecommerce business running it. No
technical detail — just what it's for.

---

## 1. Products

The master catalog of everything you sell: names, SKUs, pricing, variants
(size, color, etc.), and categories.

**Why it matters:** every other module — inventory, orders, warehouses,
analytics — refers back to this list. Keeping it accurate in one place
means your stock counts, your orders, and your reports are all talking
about the same products, instead of three different spreadsheets
slowly drifting apart.

## 2. Inventory

Tracks exactly how much of each product you have, per warehouse: how
much is on hand, how much is already promised to open orders, and how
much is actually available to sell.

**Why it matters:** this is what stops you from overselling something
you don't actually have, and from sitting on cash tied up in stock you
forgot you had. If you can't answer "how many do I have, and how much
of that is already spoken for?" without opening a spreadsheet, this is
the module that answers it instantly instead.

## 3. Warehouses

Lets you run more than one storage location — your own warehouse, a
second site, a 3PL — and see stock and capacity for each one
separately.

**Why it matters:** as soon as you store inventory in more than one
place, "how much do I have" stops being a single number. This keeps
each location's stock and utilization visible on its own, so you know
where to route an order from and when a location is getting full.

## 4. Bin Locations & Putaway

Inside each warehouse, this breaks storage down into specific shelves,
racks, and bins, and guides staff to a sensible spot when new stock
comes in — preferring a bin that already holds the same product, so
stock doesn't get scattered.

**Why it matters:** this is the fix for "the system says we have it,
but nobody can find it." Instead of staff hunting through a warehouse
from memory, every unit has a known home, which means faster picking,
fewer misplaced items, and less unexplained "lost" stock.

## 5. Stock Movements (Audit Trail)

A running log of every change to inventory — additions, removals,
transfers between warehouses, adjustments, and putaways — with who did
it and when.

**Why it matters:** when a stock count looks wrong, this is how you
trace what happened instead of guessing. It turns "our numbers don't
match" into a quick lookup rather than a warehouse-wide investigation.

## 6. Orders

The full lifecycle of a customer order: placed, paid, picked, shipped,
and fulfilled, with line items tied back to real inventory.

**Why it matters:** this is the center of the business — it's what
actually reserves stock against inventory so two customers can't be
sold the last unit twice, and it gives you one place to see every
order's status instead of chasing it across email and spreadsheets.

## 7. Customers

Customer profiles and their order history, addresses, and contact
details.

**Why it matters:** support and sales decisions are faster when you
can see a customer's full history in one place — what they've bought,
where it ships, and what's happened with past orders — instead of
piecing it together from old emails.

## 8. Analytics & Reports

Dashboards and deeper reports on sales, inventory health, and
operational performance over time.

**Why it matters:** this turns raw transaction data into the numbers
that actually drive decisions — what's selling, what's sitting, where
money is tied up — so choices about purchasing and staffing are based
on evidence instead of gut feel.

## 9. Bulk Operations, Import & Export

Make large changes — updating many products or orders at once,
importing stock or catalog data from spreadsheets, or exporting data
out for accounting, marketing, or reporting tools.

**Why it matters:** growing catalogs and growing order volumes make
one-at-a-time editing impractical. These tools are what let a small
team manage a large catalog without it becoming a full-time job, and
let data move between this system and whatever other tools the
business already relies on.

## 10. Audit Log

A record of significant actions taken in the system — who changed
what, and when — across the whole account, not just inventory.

**Why it matters:** accountability and troubleshooting. If something
unexpected happened — a price changed, an order was cancelled, a user
was removed — this is where you find out who did it and when, which
matters both for catching mistakes and for compliance.

## 11. Users & Settings

Manage who has access to the system, what they're allowed to do, and
account-wide configuration.

**Why it matters:** not everyone on the team should be able to do
everything. This lets you give staff the access they need for their
job — and nothing more — which protects the business as the team
grows.

## 12. Agents (Automation)

Configurable automated assistants that can be assigned to handle
specific kinds of work in the background.

**Why it matters:** the direction this is heading is routine,
repetitive decisions — restocking, flagging issues, routing tasks —
happening automatically instead of needing a person to notice and act
every time, freeing staff up for the exceptions that actually need a
human.

---

## The Short Version

Most ecommerce operations problems come down to the same thing: stock
information that's scattered, out of date, or simply wrong by the time
someone acts on it. Every module above exists to close one piece of
that gap — one shared, accurate picture of what you have, where it is,
what's been promised to a customer, and who touched it — so decisions
about purchasing, fulfillment, and staffing can be made on what's
actually true right now, not on a spreadsheet that was accurate last
Tuesday.
